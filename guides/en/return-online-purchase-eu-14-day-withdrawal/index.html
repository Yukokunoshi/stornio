How to return an online purchase in the EU (14-day right of withdrawal)

If you buy something online, by phone or from a seller at your door, EU law gives you 14 days to change your mind and withdraw from the contract without giving a reason. Here is how the rule works and how to use it step by step.

5 min read · Updated 2026-11-20

General information, not legal advice. National rules can differ and can change; check your country's official consumer information or a consumer advice body before you act.

When the 14-day right applies

The right of withdrawal comes from the Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU). It covers contracts you conclude with a business at a distance (online, by phone, by mail order) and contracts concluded away from the business's premises, for example at your door.

  • It applies when you buy from a trader, not from a private individual. On online marketplaces, check whether the seller is a business.
  • It does not apply to things you bought in a physical shop. Returns there depend on the shop's own policy.
  • You do not need to give a reason.

When the 14 days start

For goods, the period runs for 14 days from the day you, or someone you name (not the carrier), receive the goods. If one order arrives in several deliveries, it runs from the day the last item arrives. For services, it runs for 14 days from the day the contract is concluded.

If the last day falls on a non-working day, the deadline moves to the next working day.

If the trader did not tell you about your right of withdrawal, the period is extended by up to 12 months. If the trader gives you the information during those 12 months, you have 14 days from the day you receive it.

How to withdraw

Tell the trader clearly, before the 14 days end, that you are withdrawing from the contract. You can use the EU model withdrawal form, the trader's own form or any clear written statement, for example an e-mail. Sending a parcel back without any message is not always enough, so write it down.

Since 19 June 2026, traders that sell through a website or app must also offer an online withdrawal function (Directive (EU) 2023/2673). If the trader offers one, you can use it.

Keep proof that you withdrew in time: the sent e-mail, a screenshot of the confirmation or the postal receipt. It is up to you to show that you withdrew.

Sending the goods back and getting your money

Send the goods back within 14 days of telling the trader you are withdrawing. The trader must refund everything you paid, including the standard delivery cost, within 14 days of receiving your notice. The trader may wait to refund until the goods arrive back or you show proof that you sent them.

The refund should use the same payment method you used, unless you agree to something else.

You usually pay the direct cost of returning the goods, but only if the trader told you so before you bought. If the trader did not, or offered to pay, the trader pays.

You may handle the goods as you would in a shop to see what they are and how they work. If you use them beyond that, the trader can deduct the loss in value.

Common exceptions

The 14-day right does not apply to some purchases, for example:

  • goods made to your specifications or clearly personalised
  • goods that spoil quickly
  • sealed goods that cannot be returned for health or hygiene reasons, once unsealed
  • sealed audio, video or software, once unsealed
  • digital content you started downloading or streaming after expressly agreeing that you lose the right of withdrawal
  • accommodation, transport, car rental, catering or leisure services booked for a specific date
  • services fully performed after you expressly asked for them to start and acknowledged you would lose the right

Services and subscriptions

For a service or a subscription concluded online, the 14 days run from the day of the contract. If you asked for the service to start during the 14 days and then withdraw, you may have to pay for the part already provided.

The right exists only at the start of a new contract. For a subscription you have had for a long time, you cancel according to the contract terms instead.

Common questions

Do I have to give a reason for returning an online purchase?

No. Within the 14-day withdrawal period you do not need to give any reason.

Does the item have to be in its original packaging?

Not as such. But if you handled the goods more than needed to check them, the trader can deduct the loss in value from the refund.

What if the item is faulty?

Then you also have the legal guarantee, which lasts at least two years in the EU and does not depend on the 14-day period. Returning a faulty item is at the trader's cost.

Is the 14-day rule the same in every EU country?

The core rules are the same because they come from an EU directive, but some details can differ, for example longer periods for doorstep selling in some countries. Check your country's rules.

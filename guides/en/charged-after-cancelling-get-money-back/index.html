Charged after cancelling a subscription? How to get the money back

You cancelled, but the money still left your account. Start with the company, keep everything in writing, and know what your bank can do if the company does not react.

4 min read · Updated 2026-11-20

General information, not legal advice. National rules can differ and can change; check your country's official consumer information or a consumer advice body before you act.

1. Collect your proof

  • your cancellation and the date you sent it
  • any confirmation from the company
  • the bank or card statement showing the later charge

2. Write to the company

Write a short complaint: when you cancelled, which payment came after the end date, and that you ask for it to be refunded to the original payment method and for further charges to stop. Give a reasonable deadline for a reply and keep a copy.

3. Ask your bank

For a SEPA direct debit in euro, you can generally ask your bank for a refund within eight weeks of the debit, without giving a reason.

If a payment was not authorised, for example because you had already withdrawn the direct debit mandate, tell your bank without undue delay and at the latest within 13 months of the debit (Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366).

For card payments and debits in other currencies, the conditions depend on your bank and the card scheme. Ask your bank what applies.

4. If the company does not resolve it

You can turn to an out-of-court dispute resolution body in your country, for example a conciliation board in Hungary or a consumer conciliation body (Verbraucherschlichtungsstelle) in Germany. If the company is in another EU country, the European Consumer Centre in your country can help.

Common questions

Does the bank refund automatically?

No. You have to ask your bank, usually through online banking or a form.

Does a bank refund end my contract?

No. A refund only returns a payment. Cancel the contract separately and keep proof.

How long do I have to return something I bought in a shop?

Many people think there is always a 14-day return period. In the EU there is not, if you bought the item in a physical shop and nothing is wrong with it. Here is what applies instead.

3 min read · Updated 2026-11-20

General information, not legal advice. National rules can differ and can change; check your country's official consumer information or a consumer advice body before you act.

No general legal right for in-store purchases

The EU 14-day right of withdrawal covers purchases made online, by phone, by mail order or away from the business's premises, for example at your door. If you bought something in a shop and simply changed your mind, EU law does not give you a right to return it.

The shop's own return policy

Many shops still let you return or exchange items. That is a voluntary promise, and the shop sets the conditions.

  • Check the time limit, for example 14 or 30 days.
  • Check whether you get your money back, an exchange or only a voucher.
  • Check what the shop needs: receipt, original packaging, unused item.
  • If the policy matters to you, ask before you pay and keep the receipt or the printed policy.

If the item is faulty

If the item does not work, is damaged or is not as described, it does not matter whether you bought it in a shop or online. The legal guarantee applies: the seller is responsible for faults that existed at delivery and appear within at least two years.

You can ask the seller for a repair or a replacement free of charge. If that is not possible or fails, you can ask for a price reduction or your money back. A shop cannot refuse a faulty item just because its voluntary return period has passed.

Gifts

A gift receipt usually lets the person who received the gift exchange it under the shop's policy. The rules are again the shop's own, so check them when you buy.

Common questions

Is there a 14-day return right for things bought in a shop?

Not under general EU law. The 14-day right covers distance and off-premises purchases. In a shop, returns of non-faulty items depend on the shop's policy.

Can a shop give me a voucher instead of a refund?

For a non-faulty item returned under its voluntary policy, yes, if that is what its policy says. For a faulty item, your legal guarantee remedies apply instead.

Do I need the receipt?

Shops usually ask for it, and for a faulty item you need some proof of purchase. A receipt is the easiest; a card statement can also help.

How to keep receipts and warranty documents in one place

Most returns, repairs and refund requests start with the same question: can you show when and where you bought it? A simple habit saves a lot of searching later.

3 min read · Updated 2026-11-20

General information, not legal advice. National rules can differ and can change; check your country's official consumer information or a consumer advice body before you act.

What to keep

  • receipts and invoices for anything you might return or need repaired
  • order confirmations and delivery notes, because the delivery date matters for the 14-day period and the legal guarantee
  • contracts and terms for subscriptions, including the date you signed up
  • commercial warranty statements
  • anything you sent to cancel or complain, and the replies

A simple system

Take a photo of a paper receipt on the day you get it; thermal paper fades. Save e-mailed invoices as PDF files. Use one place, and name files with the date and the shop, for example 2026-11-20 shop-name headphones.

How long to keep them

At least as long as you might need them: until the legal guarantee and any commercial warranty have ended, and for subscriptions until the contract has ended and the last payment is settled. Limitation periods differ by country, so if in doubt keep them longer.

Common questions

Is a photo of a receipt enough?

It is usually accepted as proof of purchase, but keep the original as well when you can.

Is a card statement proof of purchase?

It can help show that you paid and when, but it does not show what you bought, so a receipt or invoice is better.
